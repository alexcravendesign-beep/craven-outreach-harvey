# Signal Playbook — Craven Cooling

A cold email from Craven earns a reply when it says who is writing and why
today, in a tradesperson's voice, and gives the venue one easy thing to do.
Scout attaches the venue's signals; this playbook turns each into the "why
today" sentence. British English throughout. No exclamation marks.

## The core rule

The signal is the reason we are writing today. Say it plainly, right after the
introduction, as something anyone could have noticed, never as something we
ran a scan for. Tie it to the season the writer is given (in autumn, December
trade; in late winter, Easter; in spring, summer). "Saw you're waiting on your first hygiene inspection" is fine. "Our
system flagged that your FHRS status is AwaitingInspection" is not. Never claim
more than the signal proves, never invent a detail, never mention the data
source by name. A wrong specific is worse than a right generic.

Every email names the venue, not a person: we write to the shared mailbox.
Second person plural is natural ("you're", "your kitchen"). Email 1 always
introduces Alex from Craven Cooling on Carmarthen Road and ends on one ask.

**The "Good" lines below show the register, not the words.** Never copy an
example sentence into an email. Write your own in that register, from the
venue's own facts. In a batch, no two emails may open the same way, and the
phrases "lets a kitchen down on the day" and "without a sales visit" are
examples here, not stock lines: use each at most once in a batch, if at all.

## Which signal leads

When a venue has several, the first line uses the first match in this order:

1. **NEW_PREMISES_LICENCE_APPLICATION** (current) — a new premises licence
   applied for: opening, or a new operator, weeks away.
2. **PLANNING_CHANGE_OF_USE** (within a year) — a new food or drink venue in
   planning.
3. **LICENCE_VARIATION** (current) — longer hours, a bigger area or a kitchen.
4. **PLANNING_REFIT** (within a year) — works at an existing venue.
5. **CH_NEW_INCORPORATION_DAYS** under 120 — a new company behind the venue.
6. **CH_OFFICER_CHANGE_DAYS** under 180 — new owner or manager, refit likely.
7. **FHRS_STRUCTURAL_SCORE** of 10 or worse — condition of equipment.
8. **FHRS_INSPECTION_AGE_DAYS** over 540 — inspection likely due.
9. **MENU_EQUIPMENT_CLASS** — what their menu says they run.
10. **VENUE_CATEGORY** alone — the kit a venue of that kind lives on.

("Awaiting first inspection" is switched off: a new registration can be a new
venue or new hands at an old one, and we will not guess which.)

Everything else (ENERGY_CLAIM, FHRS_RATING of 5, FHRS_BUSINESS_TYPE) colours
the second sentence or the follow-up; it does not lead.

## Signal → first line

### NEW_PREMISES_LICENCE_APPLICATION (a new premises licence applied for)
The council has published their application for a new licence to sell
alcohol, serve late food or put on entertainment. That happens weeks before a
venue opens, or when a new operator takes over. The notice is on the door and
in the paper, so anyone could have seen it.
- **Angle:** opening weeks away; the kit is being chosen now.
- **Good:** "Looks like {{company}} is getting ready to open. If the chilled
  kit isn't ordered yet, the showroom on Carmarthen Road has the Tefcold
  range on the floor and Lisa prices it the same day."
- **Never** say "licence", "application", "the council" or "representations".
  Never guess the opening date. If the venue is a shop (off-licence) rather
  than somewhere that serves, the angle is the drinks chiller, not a kitchen.

### PLANNING_CHANGE_OF_USE (a new venue in planning)
A planning application to turn premises into a cafe, restaurant, takeaway,
bar, pub or hotel, or to build one. Months before opening; the fit-out is
being costed now. The venue may have no name yet, so the email goes to the
applicant's or the premises' mailbox when one exists.
- **Angle:** help with the plan: what a venue like that needs, with prices.
- **Good:** "Looks like a new {{proposed use}} is coming to {{street}}. Before
  the fit-out's costed, worth knowing what the chilled kit comes to: we can
  put a priced list together from the plan, no visit needed."
- **Never** cite the planning application, the reference or the agent.

### LICENCE_VARIATION (extending hours, area or a kitchen)
An application to change an existing licence: later hours, outdoor areas,
late-night food. More trade through the same fridges, or a kitchen that did
not exist before.
- **Angle:** is the chilled kit sized for the extra trade.
- **Good:** "Looks like you're stretching the hours at {{company}}. Longer
  nights are hard on bottle coolers; if any of yours is struggling, Kyle can
  look before the busy weeks."
- **Never** mention the licence, the variation or the council.

### PLANNING_REFIT (works at an existing venue)
An extension, refurbishment, extraction, servery, terrace or bar at a pub,
restaurant, cafe, hotel, takeaway or club.
- **Angle:** tired kit gets replaced during a refit; time it with the works.
- **Good:** "Looks like you've got work planned at {{company}}. If the fridges
  are coming out while the builders are in, that's the cheap moment to swap
  anything tired; happy to price it from a photo."
- **Never** cite the planning application.

### CH_NEW_INCORPORATION_DAYS / CH_OFFICER_CHANGE_DAYS (new company or owner)
- **Angle:** a new owner usually inherits someone else's fridges.
- **Good:** "New hands on {{company}} by the look of it. If you've inherited
  the kitchen kit, worth knowing what it's costing to run before the busy weeks."
- **Never** name the director, the filing, or Companies House. We say "by the
  look of it", not "I see you were appointed on the 3rd".

### FHRS_STRUCTURAL_SCORE (condition of the building and equipment)
A poor structural score is, more often than not, chilled storage in poor nick.
- **Angle:** condition and temperature, not hygiene. Never mention the rating.
- **Good:** "If any of the chilled kit is struggling to hold temperature, Kyle
  can look at it before the busy weeks, and a replacement comes with a price
  up front."
- **Avoid:** the word "rating", the score, anything that reads as a dig.

### FHRS_INSPECTION_AGE_DAYS (inspection likely due)
- **Angle:** temperature records and reliability, offered as help.
- **Good:** "If you're due a visit this year, the two things that get asked
  about are temperature logs and whether the fridges actually hold 5°C. Happy
  to send a one-page checklist."
- Only when the age is over about 18 months; otherwise it is not a signal.

### MENU_EQUIPMENT_CLASS (what the menu names)
The main copy angle because it names the product and lets us quote a price.
| On the menu or site | They run | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| gelato, ice cream, sorbet | display freezer | the display they sell from |
| bottle bar, craft cans, wine by the glass | bottle coolers, back-bar | glass-door bottle cooler, price inline |
| deli counter, fresh fish, cured meats | serve-over | a serve-over sized to the counter |
| cakes, patisserie, bakes | display chiller | an upright cake display |
| real ale, cask | cellar cooling | cellar cooler and remote |
| takeaway, fast food | undercounter and prep | prep counter with a price |
| farm shop, butcher | serve-over and multideck | the open-front multideck |
- **Good:** "Your cake counter's doing the selling for you. If the display
  chiller ever wobbles, a Tefcold upright that size is about £X ex VAT."
- The price must come from `product_knowledge.md`; never guess one.

### VENUE_CATEGORY (nothing more specific)
Fall back to the kit a venue of that type depends on, phrased as a question.
- **Good (pub):** "How are the bottle coolers holding up behind the bar
  with the party bookings coming in?"
- **Good (cafe):** "How's the cake display coping when the room fills up?"

### ENERGY_CLAIM (they talk about energy or sustainability)
Second sentence only. "Running on R290 cuts a bottle cooler's electricity by
about a third" — only with the figure from `product_knowledge.md`.

### FHRS_RATING of 5
Second sentence, as respect: "You clearly run a tight kitchen." Never lead on
it and never mention lower ratings at all.

## Signals we do not use in copy
- **ROLE_MAILBOX, INDEPENDENT_VENUE, CH_INCORPORATED** are filters and
  compliance facts, not angles. They never appear in an email.
- **FHRS_HYGIENE_SCORE / FHRS_MANAGEMENT_SCORE** inform whether to send; they
  are never quoted or alluded to. Food safety is their business, not our pitch.

## Honesty lines
- If a prospect asks whether this is written by an AI, the answer is yes,
  straight away, every time. The email itself does not announce it. Lisa
  (sales) or Kyle (installs) takes the call.
- One email, one reason, one ask. 80 to 120 words. No links in the first
  email; the second may carry one link to a sector page.
- Stop on reply. An opt-out is honoured permanently, everywhere, immediately.
